National Academies public access file: HIMS and FADAP study

Under the Federal Advisory Committee Act, the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine maintains a public access file for each consensus study, containing materials submitted for committee review. This page indexes the public access file for Study and Recommendations on the HIMS, FADAP, and Other Drug and Alcohol Programs within the USDOT, project DBASSE-BBCSS-22-01 — the congressionally mandated review that produced the 2023 report Substance Misuse Programs in Commercial Aviation: Safety First.

The file was released by the National Academies Public Access Records Office in August 2026 and comprises 56 documents in three categories. Twenty-six are mirrored here: every document supplied by FADAP and HIMS, and both external presentations to the committee. All 26 have been verified against their contents and carry searchable text layers.

The committee-supplied portion is not mirrored. On verification, the files delivered under those names do not contain the documents the index names. The file supplied as the FAA airman medical certificate application contains a 2009 journal article on physician health programs; the file supplied as the 2022 Federal Air Surgeon Bulletin contains the FAA application form; the file supplied as “23 Years of Toxicology Testing Fatally Injured Pilots” is a byte-identical copy of the committee document index. The mismatch runs through the set.

Rather than republish documents under names that do not describe them, the committee-supplied items are listed below by title only. A corrected transmission has been requested from the Public Access Records Office. Most of that set is peer-reviewed literature retrievable from its publisher in any case; the identifiers are given below.

Why this file matters. The committee's report records that the FAA and ALPA declined, over a ten-month period, to provide HIMS outcome data, and that promised aggregate data “were never delivered.” This file shows what the committee did receive from the program: a written questionnaire response and a seminar orientation deck. It contains no database extract, no outcome dataset, no methodology documentation, and no de-identified case records.

Official project record and final report: nationalacademies.org/projects/DBASSE-BBCSS-22-01

These documents are full-text searchable. The second search box at the top of the main article searches inside all 26 mirrored documents and links straight to the page where a term appears. Two files that arrived as page images were put through optical character recognition so their text is searchable too. The index is rebuilt whenever documents are added or corrected.

The two documents that matter most

Of the 56 items, two are the program speaking in its own voice to a congressionally mandated committee, and both are cited throughout the main article.

1. HIMS program manager's written response to the committee's request for information

Quay Snyder, MD, MSPH, FAA/ALPA HIMS Program Manager. Response to questions transmitted by responsible staff officer Dylan Rebstock following the committee meeting of June 29–30, 2022; answers requested by August 23, 2022. The transmittal states that the National Academies is required to include the material in a public access file and that the answers will be made available to the public upon request.

Download PDF · Primary source

The response is organised in three parts — program structure, treatment and recovery, and partners and oversight. Points of particular evidentiary weight:

  • Asked whether there is documented permission to share database contents for research purposes even in de-identified form, the answer is “NO.”
  • The database “is not shared other than several slides shown at the HIMS seminars, but rather used internally to improve the HIMS program,” and is “reviewed by three individuals annually.”
  • Searchable fields are listed as “Age cohort, size of airline, How entered program, primary DOC, dual diagnosis, number of relapses, family history.”
  • No comprehensive program handbook exists: “NO, because each airline has their own unique program, any handbook must be tailored to that specific airline.”
  • Airlines “have agreements with a limited number of treatment center to have a waiver of medical necessity criteria and ASAM treatment criteria to have pilots admitted for a 28+ day treatment program.”
  • Treatment facility selection criteria include “willingness to waive medical necessity criteria,” admission within 24 hours, and “willingness to provide COMPLETE medical records to HIMS AME.” About a dozen facilities are used nationally.
  • 28-day inpatient treatment is given as approximately $30,000, and it is “extremely rare that an airline pilot will have IOP or less accepted as adequate treatment by the airlines or the FAA.”
  • “Without a cooperative relationship, the pilot may have an adequate medical recovery but has significant difficulty regaining medical certification and returning to work.”
  • On medication-assisted treatment: “The FAA would have to answer this question definitively as this is their policy.”
  • Asked whether the committee could review de-identified medical evaluations or progress reports: “No, not unless the FAA releases them.”

2. HIMS 2021 Advanced Topics Seminar — welcome and orientation slides

FO Craig Ohmsieder (ALPA HIMS National Chair), CPT Billy Petersen (National Vice-Chair), and Quay Snyder, MD (FAA/ALPA HIMS Program Manager). “HIMS Monitoring — Best Practices,” Westin DIA, Denver, Colorado, September 13–14, 2021.

The deck also reports overall relapse of 14.0 percent; relapse of 40.0 percent among the 25 cases whose primary drug of choice was non-specified opioids against 13.7 percent for alcohol; an age distribution stating a total of 1,261 cases whose six categories sum to 1,264; and an entry mechanism table whose nine categories sum to 1,692 cases while its stated percentages sum to 97.0 percent. It reproduces an FAA table of substance-related special issuances for calendar year 2020 totalling 2,317, of which 1,811 were first class, 240 second class, and 266 third class.

FADAP contract terms recorded in the evaluation questions response: funding comes directly from the FAA under a five-year agreement running 10 September 2020 to 9 September 2025, averaging $427,773 per year across the base and option periods, split roughly evenly between labor and non-labor costs. The contract requires the FADAP tracking database to “track all individuals who contact the program for information and referral” and requires a researcher specialising in workplace outcomes to conduct routine quality assurance and provide data interpretation.
